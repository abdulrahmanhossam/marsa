# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Marsa is a project about making deployment understandable, safe, and honest. The community around it should reflect those values. We want participation to be free of harassment, intimidation, and discrimination, regardless of experience level, language, nationality, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, or technical preference.

This code applies to the repository, issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, chat spaces, and any project event or representation.

## Expected behavior

- Be direct about technical problems and generous with the people solving them.
- Critique the design, code, claim, or decision — not the person.
- Ask for context before assuming bad intent.
- Welcome questions from beginners, including questions about tools that experienced contributors consider obvious.
- Respect language differences. Arabic and English are both first-class project languages; do not mock someone’s wording or accent.
- Give credit for ideas and work.
- Keep disagreement focused on evidence, user impact, and project scope.
- Accept a maintainer’s decision to stop a thread that has become personal or repetitive.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, stalking, or sustained unwanted contact.
- Insults, slurs, demeaning jokes, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination based on any protected or personal characteristic.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or attention in project spaces.
- Publishing someone’s private information, including home address, phone number, private email, employer, or account details, without permission.
- Deliberately exposing credentials, private repository URLs, customer data, or security details.
- Intimidating or retaliating against someone who reports a concern.
- Spamming, deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly ignoring scope decisions.
- Presenting copied work as original, or encouraging others to violate licenses or clean-room rules.

## Scope

This code applies when you are representing the project or participating in a project space. It also applies when your behavior outside the project creates a direct, credible safety risk for participants.

Technical disagreement is not a conduct violation. A firm review, a rejected proposal, or a request to reduce scope is not harassment. Conduct concerns are about how people are treated, not whether their idea was accepted.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness a conduct issue:

1. Prefer a private channel. Contact the repository owner or a maintainer through the contact method on their GitHub profile.
2. If the concern involves a maintainer or you cannot find a private channel, use GitHub’s abuse-reporting mechanism and ask the project owner to establish a private channel.
3. Do not post sensitive details, screenshots of private messages, or personal information in a public issue.
4. Include what happened, where, when, who was involved, and whether there is an immediate safety risk. Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

Security vulnerabilities are handled separately under [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).

## Confidentiality and retaliation

Reports will be shared only with the people needed to assess and respond, to the extent practical. We will not promise absolute confidentiality when safety or legal obligations require otherwise, but we will not publish a report or the reporter’s identity without consent.

Retaliation against a reporter or participant in an investigation is itself a violation and may lead to removal from project spaces.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will assess the report, may ask for clarification, and may take proportionate action:

1. **Private clarification** — explain the impact and ask for a change in behavior.
2. **Warning** — a recorded warning with expected behavior.
3. **Temporary restriction** — time-limited removal from one or more project spaces.
4. **Permanent removal** — removal from project spaces for severe or repeated violations.

Urgent safety issues may lead to immediate temporary restrictions while the report is reviewed. Maintainers should disclose any conflict of interest and recuse themselves when appropriate.

## Good-faith reporting

Reports made in good faith are protected even if the facts are not fully established. Knowingly false reports, coordinated harassment through reporting, and attempts to weaponize this process are violations.

## Changes

This code may be updated as the project grows. Material changes require maintainer review and a note in the pull request. The current version applies to all project spaces at the time of the behavior.
